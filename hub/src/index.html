What we verified on the bench

The combined sketch was compiled and uploaded on October 24. Live serial confirmed movement detection, clear rearming and effect completion. LEDs worked after correcting the input end and repairing contacts. The current effect is a continuous spectrum rainbow with a sparkling ripple from the center toward both ends.

The final refinement has smoother color blending and a 32-second rainbow cycle. State tests cover valid-only debounce, rearming, invalid and stale readings, sampling gaps and timer wrap. Renderer tests cover symmetry and propagation. The physical LED count is still an estimate; 90 is the configured count.

Not yet tested: multi-sensor operation, directional detection, installed cable runs, permanent mounting or a finished power system. One sensor currently detects presence, not travel direction.

01 / NEXT VISIT

Get four to five sensors
working with the LEDs.

This is the chosen prototype goal. We’ll add sensors one at a time, then test them as a group before moving on to installation planning.

  1. Set up the multiplexer bench.

    Label the sensors 1–5. Use one VL53L1X per TCA9548A channel. Verify the actual carrier supply, logic levels and connectors, then select and initialize each channel independently.

  2. Connect the sensing to the lights.

    Build a separate mux-aware firmware version. Confirm each channel’s reading and LED response, clear/rearm behavior and repeated cold boots. The current downloadable sketch supports one sensor directly on Wire.

  3. Prove the complete group.

    Run all 4–5 sensors with LEDs active. Check timing, overlapping views, false or missed detections, lingering presence, invalid readings and recovery. Agree the trial count and record actual results.

Ready to move on when: every intended channel works after restart, readings stay attributable to the right sensor, and repeated approaches produce the intended LED behavior without false triggers.
Decisions and measurements to capture that day

Choose whether the final behavior needs presence, ordered travel direction or individual step coverage. Survey tread count, widths, riser heights, landing geometry, sightlines, mounting depth and cable routes. Confirm the real strip count and specification. Keep household measurements in the on-site notes, not the public guide.

Selecting a mux channel does not stop other powered sensors from ranging optically. Compare isolated and grouped operation; validate staggered measurements if needed, polling latency, ambient-light changes, target differences and simultaneous traffic.

03 / THE CONNECTION PLAN

One controller.
Separate sensor channels.

The multiplexer is the chosen splitter. It separates sensors with the same address. This is a conceptual bench topology, not a verified terminal-by-terminal installation drawing.

The signal path

UNO main SDA/SCL → TCA9548A → one sensor per channel.

Use CH0–CH3 for four sensors and CH4 for the optional fifth. Each sensor stays at address 0x29; the mux defaults to 0x70. Firmware selects exactly one channel before configuring or reading that sensor.

Never enable several same-address sensor channels at once. Keep readings and presence state separate for each sensor.

Power and ground

A verified regulated sensor rail feeds the carriers. Ground is shared between UNO, mux and sensors. Check the group’s load before deciding whether the controller rail can power it.

LED 5 V supply → appropriate fused distribution → strip power and planned injection points. D6 → series resistor → first DIN, with a shared signal ground. Strip current never passes through the UNO or a breadboard.

The diagram does not connect the external LED +5 V to the USB-powered UNO +5 V.

Short bench first. Real routes next.

A mux is not a long-cable extender. Measure the proposed sensor routes and test the intended cable lengths, bus pull-ups, capacitance and rise time before hiding anything.

Controller location, branch lengths, exact pin assignments and permanent connector choices remain TBD. Revise the topology if raw I²C cannot meet timing over the measured route.

Electrical compatibility and manufacturer references

The chosen HiLetgo offer’s pull-up values and rail connections are unverified. Inventory both mux and carrier pull-ups before adding resistors. Adafruit’s guide documents a different breakout, including its absent downstream pull-ups; do not transfer that board-specific claim to HiLetgo. TI’s device documentation explains the selectable channels and rail requirements.

The CQRobot CQRWX00744US manual specifies 3.3 V/5 V operation and onboard voltage translation. Match the actual carrier revision and labels; this does not prove every signal pin is 5 V tolerant. The proposed combination still needs an end-to-end UNO R4 bench test.

The mux plan does not need XSHUT address sequencing. Do not assume 5 V on SHUT/INT. Pololu’s carrier documentation warns its XSHUT is not 5 V tolerant; its schematic is not a substitute for CQRobot’s. A shared-bus address-assignment alternative would require individually controlled startup after each reset.

NXP’s I²C specification is the basis for bus timing and capacitance checks. There is no universal cable length prescribed here. A buffered/differential link or local-controller redesign may be needed after testing.

04 / THE FULL STAIRCASE

Estimate the route.
Measure before ordering.

Gary estimates 15–18 steps. The ranges below are planning examples, not surveyed dimensions or an approved installation.

Assumed only: 28 cm tread, 18 cm riser, 90 cm lit width per step, no landing, 1.5 m enclosure approach and 15% routing allowance plus 0.3 m service length per sensor. The earlier 1,045 mm sensor background is not a stair-width measurement.
Main raceway · two route options

8–9 m sloped
or 10–12 m stepped

A straight side route is about 5.0–6.0 m. Following every tread and riser is about 6.9–8.3 m. Add the assumed 1.5 m approach and 15% allowance to get the rounded main-raceway ranges.

These are alternatives, not quantities to add together. Landings, corners, access gaps and sensor branches are extra. Allow 10–20% after measuring the actual route; choose inside/outside corners and end fittings then.

LED channel and diffuser

13.5–16.2 m of lit width

15–18 steps × assumed 0.90 m. Each channel and diffuser must be cut to its actual tread; allow roughly 16–19 m of material with provisional cut allowance, not a final order quantity. Verify strip cut spacing and mounting depth first.

This is far more lighting than the 90-pixel bench. At an assumed 60 pixels/m it would mean 810–972 pixels; density and total remain unverified.

LED data and power

Different jobs, different sizing.

Data follows D6 → first DIN → each next strip’s DIN with a reference ground. All DIN ends on one side can require return jumpers across each tread. Alternating strip direction can shorten jumps but needs routes on both sides; do not combine that assumption with one-side-only raceway. Exact length depends on which ends join; it is not automatically the raceway length. Validate the longest gap with LEDs under load; use a suitable buffered or differential data link if needed.

Power uses fused branches or an engineered distribution trunk with injection. Total cable depends on branch count and feed location. Measure each one-way run; its electrical loop includes both positive and return conductors.

Permanent connections

Secure, labelled and reachable.

Use stranded copper conductors, connectors rated for the chosen wire and branch current, and keyed sensor harnesses with a verified pin order. Retain owned Wagos only where their exact model and conductor ratings fit. Soldered strip pigtails need insulation and independent strain relief; do not bury loose jumpers or pressure-only strip clips.

Mount raceway and removable diffusers securely, keep junctions accessible, and place service loops inside enclosures. Decide controller access, supply ventilation, entry glands and the proposed through-hole after the on-site survey and hidden-utility check.

Power budget

The 50 W adapter is bench inventory.

5 V × 10 A = 50 W. If the actual strip label is 90 W per 5 m, that is 18 W/m: a 5 m reel needs 18 A at full rated load, while 13.5–16.2 m would be 243–292 W / 49–58 A. These are conditional label calculations, not measured consumption.

Verify the real strip specification and intended maximum pattern. Choose supply capacity with manufacturer derating, branch fuses, conductor sizes and injection from that load. Brightness 16 is not wiring protection.

Bus qualification, voltage drop and the design gate

Sensor link: start bench qualification at 100 kHz, inventory pull-ups and rails, then check rise time and total capacitance for each selected path including the upstream bus. Standard-mode limits include 1 µs rise time and generally 400 pF per line; lowering the clock does not repair bad voltage levels or unlimited capacitance. NXP bus specification · TI pull-up calculations.

If actual branches fail, assess compatible bus buffers, a differential link per isolated sensor branch, or distributed controllers with a robust interconnect. NXP’s PCA9615 is one differential architecture to evaluate: it needs devices at both ends, suitable pairs and termination, and has its own cable/speed limits. It is not a promise that an 8 m branch works or a drop-in shopping recommendation.

Power calculation: voltage drop ≈ 2 × one-way length × current × conductor resistance per metre. For illustration, a cable specified at 0.021 Ω/m over 3 m one-way drops about 0.25 V at 2 A or 0.63 V at 5 A, before contacts and strip copper. At 5 V this is significant. Select the actual copper gauge using its data sheet, temperature/installation ratings and allowed drop; check far-end voltage under load. Fuse each branch to protect its weakest conductor or connector, within supply and fuse time-current limits. Injection spacing follows those results, not a fixed number of steps.

Before installation: measure routes, width and each branch; identify strip density/rating; pass the 4–5 sensor trial and full-length link tests; approve load, distribution and secure mounting. Then install a small reversible pilot and repeat traffic, restart, lingering and failure tests. LED power and injection reference.
