01 / NEXT VISIT

Resolve the fault.
Then prove one branch.

The eight-sensor system is a plan, not a tested installation. There is no need to repeat the same resistance test without a reason; the reported isolated-board results are recorded.

  1. Choose repair investigation or a replacement mux.

    The reported VIN–SDA/SCL/RST readings make the current board suspect. Leave it out of the powered system until the issue is understood. A new known-compatible board is a practical next step, not proof that the original cause was an internal chip failure.

  2. Review the actual sensor and strip connections.

    Confirm board labels, rails, connector pin order, pull-ups and shared ground. Keep LED current out of the UNO and breadboard. The roughly 80-LED estimate needs a physical count. The compiled interference diagnostic has not been uploaded; any upload is a separate hardware action.

  3. Restore a short, known-good single-sensor baseline.

    After the wiring and mux are qualified, prove one sensor on one selected channel before adding channels. Preserve the archived direct-Wire 90-LED source and the separate diagnostic. A scan alone does not prove channel switching or reliable distance readings.

  4. Measure before cutting or multiplying the setup.

    Choose the controller location, measure all eight actual routes, and test the longest planned sensor cable first. Try one removable sensor housing and an under-rail light section. Verify sensor height, angle, passage coverage, connector clearance and optical opening before drilling.

  5. Qualify all eight sensors with the lights.

    Test selected channels separately, then the full sequence under intended LED load, repeated cold boots, simultaneous traffic, missed readings and recovery. Confirm optical interference and far-end voltage. Long raw I²C links are not proven by adding a mux.

Installation gates remain open: known-good channel switching, reliable sensing at the actual cable lengths, final LED count and load, fused power distribution, and a safe removable mounting trial.

Logical connection concept, not a pin-level wiring plan. Each branch has VCC, GND, SDA and SCL on the proposed four-conductor cable. Exact carrier pin order and rail compatibility still need checking.

Keep identical addresses isolated.

Use one sensor on each mux channel CH0–CH7. Select exactly one channel before configuring or reading that sensor. Never enable several same-address channels together.

A different scheme requires verified address assignment and additional control wiring where needed. Do not assume tying eight unchanged sensors directly in parallel will work.

Separate signal and LED power design.

Use a verified regulated sensor rail and common signal ground. LED current must not pass through the UNO or a breadboard. LED power, injection feeds and fuses need their own load calculation.

The archived sketch uses D6 through a series resistor to strip DIN. Current strip/sensor wiring is pending review; the historic pin setting is not evidence of the present connection.

Raw I²C length is an open risk.

A mux separates addresses; it is not a long-cable extender. Qualify the longest actual branch with the intended cable, pull-ups, bus voltage and LED load before duplicating eight runs.

At 100 kHz, standard-mode limits include 1 µs rise time and generally 400 pF per line. Lower clock speed does not fix shorts or wrong voltage levels. Buffered/differential links or distributed controllers may be needed if raw I²C fails.

800 mm is a saved trigger setting.

The archived ToF firmware uses 800 mm to trigger and 950 mm to exit/rearm. These are saved software thresholds, not the sensor’s maximum range and not confirmed settings in the current setup.

Do not carry thresholds from the older reflective sensors into the new ToF setup as measured coverage. Test the actual enclosure, angle and target conditions.

Manufacturer references and electrical limits

TI TCA9548A data sheet · NXP I²C specification · CQRobot CQRWX00744US manual.

Breakout-board pull-ups, rail connections and revisions must be checked on the actual hardware. A chip data sheet or another brand’s schematic does not prove this board’s wiring. Do not assume SHUT/INT is 5 V tolerant; the mux approach does not require XSHUT address sequencing.

Sensor cable quantity

57 ft bare → about 75 ft.

Exact bare total: 56 ft 8 in. Add eight 1 ft service allowances, then 15% spare: (56.67 + 8) × 1.15 = 74.37 ft, rounded to about 75 ft of four-conductor cable.

This means 75 ft of cable containing four wires, about 300 conductor-feet. It does not mean four separate 75-ft cables per sensor. LED power/data wiring is excluded.

Controller location matters

Each extra foot adds 9.2 ft.

If every branch has one additional foot between the stair end and controller, eight cables × 1 ft × 1.15 adds 9.2 ft total. A 3 ft approach would raise this example to about 102 ft.

General formula: 1.15 × (sum of eight measured routed distances + 8 ft service allowance). A midpoint controller changes the routes and the estimate. Measure before ordering or cutting.

Under-rail lighting

Measure the light route separately.

The selected direction is a strip beneath the handrail and sensors along the baseboard, not a separate light strip on every tread. Measure rail length, bracket interruptions, first DIN path and each power feed separately.

Approximately 80 LEDs is only an estimate. It is not a confirmed length, count or power load. Confirm strip type, cut points, diffuser profile, glare and mounting clearance.

Sensor boxes

Mock up one before eight.

Use the actual 28.5 × 23 mm rectangular CQRobot board as the reference. The 60 × 36 × 17 mm box candidate needs a connector-depth check, optical aperture and cable opening.

Keep the handrail grip and walking area clear. Test safe projection above carpet and real passage at near, center and far-side positions. The reported width does not prove sensor coverage.

Power is not sized yet

5 V / 10 A is bench inventory.

The owned adapter is rated 50 W. Its suitability for the final strip, brightness, feeds and cable gauge is unverified. The old 90-configured-LED success does not validate full-installation power.

Count LEDs, identify strip ratings, measure the intended load and far-end voltage, and select suitable fuses, connectors and injection. Brightness settings are not wiring protection.

Before installation

Confirm the eight real paths.

Choose controller placement, confirm stair measurement endpoints, and measure each sensor cable, LED feed, rail bracket bypass and raceway entry. Eight evenly spaced points are a calculation assumption, not final step assignments.

No mains wiring or structural rail changes are specified. Keep service connections accessible and cables protected from abrasion.
