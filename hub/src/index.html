WHAT THE SKETCH DOES

Two inputs. Two directions.

Sensor 1 then sensor 2, within 2 seconds, calls the up animation. Sensor 2 then sensor 1 calls the down animation.

Up uses a blue-to-violet hue range. Down uses the sketch’s magenta-to-orange/red hue mapping. Both use a five-LED head and a fading tail.

CURRENT LIMITS

A prototype, preserved.

75 LEDs are allocated; only the first 50 are animated. Each frame delays 25 ms, so sensors are not polled during an animation.

No Wi-Fi implementation or 15-sensor implementation is present in this revision. This is the original revision; the separate current bench firmware is available above.

CONFIRMED IN CODE

Digital, active-low inputs.

D2 and D3 use INPUT, not INPUT_PULLUP. The sketch treats LOW as triggered. It does not establish a sensor voltage, output type or external biasing circuit.

The LED type is WS2812B, driven from D6 using FastLED with GRB color order.

Project photos identify the Arduino UNO R4 WiFi and four-pin HW-870 reflective IR modules labeled VCC, GND, DO and AO with a blue threshold pot. Gary counts fourteen modules, with two currently connected. DO polarity and AO response direction still need observation.

These pictured modules are close-range TCRT5000-family reflectance sensors, not time-of-flight devices. Six additional three-pin LDR ambient-light threshold modules are a different component. No private project photos are published.

LED strip & power inventory

WS2812E bag: 5V, 5m, 90W. YS-0510DC adapter: 5V, 10A, 50W. The labeled full reel requires 90W ÷ 5V = 18A, exceeding the adapter’s 10A rating. Do not route LED current through the UNO or a breadboard.

The original sketch specifies WS2812B; the new bag says WS2812E. Do not assume protocol, connector or wiring compatibility from that label alone. During the October 24 bench, the data connection was moved to the correct input end and contact faults were repaired; LEDs then worked. Installed power distribution and worst-case load remain undesigned.

Available: a 1000µF / 16V capacitor kit and a visible loose 1000µF / 10V capacitor. Keep capacitors disconnected until wiring and polarity are verified. No capacitor connection instructions are established here.

What was verified, and what remains open
  • UNO R4 WiFi, Arduino Renesas core 1.6.0, FastLED 3.10.5 and Pololu VL53L1X 1.3.1.
  • LEDs worked after correcting the input end and repairing connections. The one-sensor combined firmware was uploaded and produced real sensor events.
  • Final smooth version compiled: 71,480 bytes flash (27%), 9,944 bytes RAM (30%). Upload wrote 71,488 binary bytes in 18 pages.
  • State tests cover valid-only debounce, rearming, invalid readings, stale recovery, sample gaps and timer wrap. Renderer tests cover symmetry, propagation and smooth color phase.
  • One sensor detects presence, not direction. Nothing is mounted; 4–5 sensor operation, installed cable runs, whole-stair coverage and final power design are not tested.
Live bench evidence and threshold behavior

After the final upload, serial reported:

ARMED: View is clear. Approach within 800 mm to launch a ripple.
MOVEMENT DETECTED: Center ripple scattering toward both ends.
EFFECT COMPLETE: Ripple blended back into the rainbow.

Enter below 800 mm requires at least 200 ms and three valid samples. Clear above 950 mm requires at least 400 ms and five valid samples. A held presence cannot repeatedly trigger. Invalid samples do not mean zero distance or a clear view; over one second without valid data disarms detection until clear is confirmed.

Background distance changed with sensor aim, so these are bench thresholds to tune in the actual geometry. Invalid range status 7 was observed earlier, and valid readings later recovered. No permanent hardware cause was established.

NEXT VISIT / THE PLAN

Four to five sensors.
Then the fit.

The next milestone is 4–5 VL53L1X sensors working together with the LEDs through a multiplexer. Installation planning follows that proof.

Prototype target, not an installed system

Sensor quantity is the chosen trial scope. Final positions, required coverage, cable lengths and installed power remain decisions to validate.

01

DEFINE + SURVEY

Agree what detection should mean.

Presence, ordered travel direction, or individual step coverage? Record tread count, widths, riser heights, landing geometry, mounting depth, sensor sightlines and cable routes. Confirm the real LED count and strip specification.

Exit gate and decisions

Choose the behavior and a measurable success criterion before selecting positions. Keep private household measurements in the on-site worksheet, not the public site. No HW-870 tape-across-stairs assumption.

02

MULTIPLEXER BENCH / NEXT VISIT GOAL

Make all 4–5 sensors work with LEDs.

Use one VL53L1X per channel on a TCA9548A breakout. Check supply and bus logic first, enumerate channels, then select and initialize each sensor individually. Integrate valid readings into the current nonblocking LED controller.

Exit gate and bring-up checklist

Every intended channel identifies its own sensor at 0x29 after repeated cold boots. No combined same-address channels are enabled. Each sensor can trigger the intended LED response, and its label matches the physical module. Ordered events are logged if direction is part of the agreed behavior.

The current download supports one directly connected sensor only. A mux-aware multi-sensor firmware revision must be built and tested; it is not already implemented.

03

TIMING + REPEATABILITY

Prove the group behaves reliably.

Compare isolated and grouped operation, overlapping views, staggered ranging, different targets, ambient light, repeated approaches, simultaneous people, lingering presence and LED activity.

Exit gate and fault tests

Agree the trial count and tolerated missed/false detections on site; log actual outcomes. Check worst-case polling latency with 4–5 sensors rather than multiplying today’s 60 ms period blindly. Selecting an I²C channel does not stop other powered sensors from ranging optically. Validate scheduling and interference separately.

Test invalid readings, a disconnected sensor, restart and recovery without false triggers. Save a labelled log and the exact firmware revision before moving on.

04

PLACEMENT + ENCLOSURES

Design the parts that disappear.

Mock up LED channels and diffusers, sensor apertures and aiming, removable mounts, and an accessible controller enclosure. Route hidden wiring with labels, slack for service, strain relief and protected transitions.

Exit gate

Approve a reversible placement mock-up with clear sightlines and no foot-contact or snagging hazards. Test the sensor through any proposed cover window; do not assume a diffuser or dark plastic is optically harmless. Controller and junctions must remain reachable.

05

POWER + CABLING

Design for the real load and route.

Use the actual strip specification, measured lengths and chosen maximum brightness to size the 5 V supply, branch wiring, fuses, connectors and injection points. Check voltage drop at the far end under the intended worst-case pattern.

Exit gate and electrical record

Document the load budget, wire gauge, fuse coordination, polarity, return paths and injection layout. Brightness 16 is a bench setting, not a substitute for protected wiring. LED current goes directly from its supply through appropriate distribution, never through the UNO or breadboard.

Test proposed sensor cable lengths, pull-up loading, capacitance and rise time. A mux is not a long-distance line driver. If runs cannot meet I²C timing, revise the topology with an appropriate buffered/differential link or local controller design before installation. No cable gauge, fuse value or maximum run is guessed here.

06

PILOT + ACCEPTANCE

Install a small section. Walk it.

Fit a reversible pilot only after the bench and design gates pass. Repeat foot-traffic, lingering, opposite-direction, ambient-light and failure tests in the actual positions before extending the installation.

Exit gate

Confirm service access, secure mounting, absence of exposed conductors, labelled connections, reliable restart and a documented recovery sketch. Expand only after the agreed pilot criteria pass and the installation design is approved.

CHOSEN ARCHITECTURE / CONCEPT LAYOUT

One controller.
Separate channels.

The multiplexer is the right kind of splitter for repeated sensor addresses: one sensor per selected channel. This is a bench topology and installation concept, not a verified terminal-by-terminal wiring plan.

Architecture selected

A mux, not a passive fan-out.

Recommend the documented Adafruit TCA9548A 1-to-8 I²C Multiplexer Breakout as the provisional bench part. It supports a 5 V Arduino host; its eight channel pairs separate identical-address devices. Check the actual breakout and sensor carrier before assigning rails.

The VL53L1X default address is 0x29. On each operation, firmware selects exactly one channel at the mux’s default 0x70 address, then initializes, configures or reads that channel’s sensor. Keep per-sensor state. Repeat initialization after reset; never enable several 0x29 channels together.

Power, logic and pull-ups before exact wiring

The CQRobot CQRWX00744US manual specifies 3.3 V/5 V operation and onboard voltage translation. Confirm the actual carrier SKU/revision and connector labels; this does not establish that every signal pin is 5 V tolerant.

For the documented Adafruit mux, the guide specifies matching VIN to host logic for a 5 V Arduino. Downstream channel pairs have no pull-ups installed on that breakout. Inventory the carriers’ existing pull-ups and rail levels, then establish correct pull-ups per selected segment; do not blindly add resistors or connect incompatible rails.

Use a verified regulated sensor supply and shared ground; check the complete 4–5 sensor load before deciding whether the controller rail can supply it. This diagram does not join the external LED +5 V to the USB-powered UNO +5 V.

XSHUT is not needed to solve addresses in this mux plan

Individual channels isolate the identical sensor addresses, so the primary plan does not require five Arduino shutdown lines. Keep SHUT/INT in their documented default state until the actual carrier is verified.

If a future design instead assigns unique addresses on one shared bus, it needs controlled individual startup and address assignment after each reset. Pololu explicitly warns its XSHUT is not 5 V tolerant; do not transfer a 5 V supply claim to CQRobot SHUT or assume identical carrier circuitry. Verify the carrier’s signal limits first.

Bus selection is not optical scheduling

A mux separates the I²C communication channels. It does not automatically stop other powered ToF emitters. The 4–5 sensor trial must compare grouped operation and deliberately stagger measurement starts if needed, measuring latency and invalid readings with LEDs active.

POWER + PHYSICAL ROUTING / NOT TO SCALE

Keep power deliberate. Keep service possible.

USB → UNO + mux area

Controller near an accessible landing or service location, chosen after measuring. Short upstream bus; labelled channel branches.

Regulated sensor supply → sensor branches

Supply rating, logic rails and carrier pins verified first. Shared ground; protected, labelled connectors.

5 V LED supply → fused distribution → LED + / GND

LED power goes directly to appropriately sized wiring and planned injection points. UNO D6 → series resistor → first DIN; common signal ground.

Hidden route → removable mounts → sensor/LED positions

Proposed channels, enclosure, strain relief and service loops. Exact lengths, mounting points, feed locations and diffuser depth: TBD.

A complete installed power plan needs measured load, wire gauge, branch protection, voltage-drop checks and connector ratings. Never carry strip current through the UNO or a breadboard.

Short bench wiring first. Actual cable runs next.

Do not assume a raw I²C branch will work over the full staircase. Cable capacitance, pull-ups, noise and rise time determine the usable wiring, not a universal metre limit. Record each route and test at its intended length before hiding it. Choose a revised buffered/differential or local-controller topology if the measured bus cannot meet timing.
